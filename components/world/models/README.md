# Building a landmark

Every place on the planet has a landmark, modelled in code from the kit in
`kit.tsx`. This is how to build one so that it holds up close. The bar, from
the spec: **a close-up screenshot of any landmark should look like a frame
from a stylised anime film, not a toy.** The first sketch was rejected as
"childish/cartoony": blocky toy shapes, thick black outlines, primary colours.
The kit makes the better result the easy one; these rules keep it that way.

`GenericModel.tsx` is the worked example. Read it before starting.

## The frame you are working in

- Model units. The ground is y = 0, +Y is up, and **+Z is the front**: south,
  toward the camera as it arrives. Put the door, the sign, the face on +Z.
- `OnGround` (in `Places.tsx`) scales a landmark by `MODEL_SCALE` (0.088 world
  units per model unit) and stands it on its place.
- **Footprint:** stay within your place's **allowed radius** of the origin
  (solid geometry only: light, such as a `Beam` or a `Signal`, is exempt)
  across the ground, and no taller than **2.6** (`FOOTPRINT` in the kit). The
  allowed radius is 1.3 model units, or half the distance to the nearest
  neighbouring place where that is less (`footprintRadius`), so no two
  landmarks' footprints overlap. Trees and background buildings keep wholly
  outside every place's allowed radius (`lib/world/scatter.ts`). In development, `Batched` (wrapped round every landmark by
  `Places`) checks the landmark after every merge (`checkFootprint`): it
  warns in the console if any vertex leaves the allowed radius, and logs the
  landmark's triangle count (see the budget below), once per change. The
  table is generated by `footprintTable`, and a test fails if it goes stale;
  if a place moves in `content/world.ts`, regenerate it. Places stay at least
  9 degrees apart and every allowed radius at least 1.0 (both tested), so a
  place moved too close fails a test rather than quietly shrinking a
  neighbour's footprint.

- **Height:** build to `place.height` (in `content/world.ts`). The camera's
  framing (`focusPose`) and the label above the landmark are placed from it,
  so a landmark much shorter or taller than its place says will be framed
  badly. If the design needs another height, change the place's `height`.
  Every part that depends on the height follows it, not just the top one:
  state the range the landmark is built for (`MyLandmark.heights = [1.0,
  1.6]`), take the place's height through `heightWithin`, and derive the rest
  from it (walls as a share of it, a tower's shaft as what the parts above it
  leave). The landmark test mounts it at both ends of its range. Build tall
  things bottom up with `stackUp`, and stand anything on a cone or a turned
  part on its true top (`peakTop`, `turnedTop`): a rounded tip stands lower
  than its nominal height, and a pole set on the nominal one floats. Lay a
  branch, a leg or a pipe from one point to another with `pointing(from, to)`,
  which gives a `Tube` its place, turn and length.
- A landmark is a component taking `{ place, district }` (`ModelProps`),
  registered under its model key in `registry.ts`.

## The rules

1. **Proportions before detail.** Block out the main masses first and look
   at them from the camera. Real objects have proportions: doors about twice
   as tall as wide, storeys about 0.45–0.5 units here, roofs a third of a small
   building's height. A wrong mass cannot be saved by detail.
2. **Bevel every edge a real object has.** The parts do this by default
   (`Block` 0.04, round parts 0.03, plates 0.012). Do not turn bevels off to
   make something look crisp; make the bevel smaller. The bevels carry the
   edge light, which is most of what lifts a shape from a toy block.
3. **Three levels of detail on every landmark:**
   - the **main mass** (walls, a tower, a tent, a mountain);
   - **secondary forms**: roofs, doors, windows, trim bands, frames, steps,
     railings;
   - **small props that reward a close-up**: a lamp, a sign, a planter, a
     cat, a mailbox flag.
   A landmark with only the first level is a toy.
4. **Colour only from the `landmark` palette** (`content/sky.ts`) **and the
   district's colour** (`district.color`). Never a hex value in a component.
   Mix them with `blend(a, b, k)`: a light wall is the district colour blended
   toward `cream`; a deep roof or door is it blended toward `indigo`. Use the
   pure district colour sparingly, for accents. Blend toward `indigo`, never
   toward black, to darken.
5. **The front faces +Z**, and the landmark should also read from the sides
   and back: the camera can circle it.
6. **Stay in the footprint** (your place's allowed radius, at most 1.3; 2.6 tall).
7. **Windows are `Glass`** (a `Window` has it already): sky by day, lit at
   night. **Things that shine use `Lit`**: lamps, signs, screens. Nothing
   else glows; the bloom is for things that are actually bright.
8. **Three things come free; do not fight them.** Every landmark stands in a
   soft contact shadow, every kit surface is painted a little deeper and
   cooler at the ground and lighter toward the top, and every window pane and
   lamp follows the time of day. Do not paint fake shadows or gradients on
   top: no dark strips under eaves, no darker bands near the ground. (The
   generic building had a painted eaves shadow until the gradient and the
   contact shadow came; it has been taken out.)

## Which part to reach for

radians), a `color`, and `outline={false}` to drop the line. The cost is
about how many triangles a typical one draws, its outline included (roughly
halve it with `outline={false}`); it is what the budget below counts. Every
figure is measured from the part it names (`kitCosts.test.tsx`), so a figure
that drifts fails a test; change the figure there and here together. A figure
written here with no measurement there fails too, so a new part's row comes
with its entry in that test.

Identical parts share one geometry automatically (a cache keyed by their
parameters), so repeat a window twenty times freely. Sizes that differ by
less than 0.0001 share.

Round parts pick their own number of sides from their size (`roundSides`:
about 15 for a pot, 20 for a tower, up to 40 for a big dome); pass `sides`
only to make something deliberately faceted (8 or fewer) or smoother.

No prop value breaks a part. Sizes below zero or not numbers count as zero,
and a part with no solid to it (no height, a flat roof, no width) draws
nothing rather than throwing. A bevel of 0 still rounds edges by `MIN_BEVEL`
(0.002, too small to see), because the outline and the shading need a true
normal at every edge; it looks sharp. Side counts are whole numbers from 3
to 64, and a roof gets at most 40 courses to a slope. A shaped plate whose
outline encloses no area, or that has no depth, draws nothing, and its bevel
is kept under half its depth and a quarter of its narrowest side. A ball
squashed flat on an axis draws nothing. All of this is swept by tests over
every part (`kitRobustness.test.ts`, `kitParts.test.ts`).

Do not scale a kit part, or a group of them, to size it: the outline and the
bevels scale with it. Give the part its real size instead.

## Outlines

Lines are thin and dark indigo (`terrain.outline`), never black, never thick.
Every part draws its own: a copy of the part drawn on its back faces, a set
width outside the surface. Where two parts meet, the line shows the seam.

The width follows the part's size (`lineWidth`): a part at least `LINE.fullAt`
(0.1) across, by its middle extent, gets the full line (`OUTLINE.part`, 0.009
units, about a pixel and a half at a place); a smaller one a thinner line, in
proportion, down to `LINE.floor` (0.4) of it, in `LINE.steps` (4) steps so a
landmark's lines share a few materials. A knob, a rail post or a cat is not
drawn as heavily as a wall, which is much of what reads as a toy. Every part
does this for itself; nothing to set.

| Parts | How the line is made |
| --- | --- |
| `Block`, `Tube`, `Peak`, `Die`, `Turned`, `Striped` (round its whole shape), `SnowPeak` (its smooth shell, round the whole peak), `Rounded`, `Tufted`, `Roof`, `Ring`, `Swept` (as fine as it is broad), `Truss` (as fine as its bars), `Lit`, `Glass`, `Brickwork` (its slab; its bricks draw none) | the part's own geometry pushed out along its normals in the vertex shader (`outlineShellMaterial`). Exactly even, for long, thin, flat or hollow parts too, because every edge is bevelled and the normals are continuous (a `Block` is a chamfered box whose chamfers carry its faces' normals, `chamferedBox`) |
| `Blob` | a scaled copy, `outlineScale(radius, width)` per axis: a sphere's offset is a bigger sphere |
| `Extruded` | a second extrusion grown by the line's width: its flat-shaded sides would crack a pushed line |
| `Flag` | a grown copy of the cloth on its back faces, rippled by the same vertex shader |
| `Picture` | none (it sits on a frame you build) |
| `Beam`, `Signal`, `Flame`, `Water`, `Rain`, `RainCloud` | none: light has no edge, water sits in a rim you build, and rain and its cloud are painted as the sky's clouds are, with no line |

`outlineScale` is the rule for compact convex parts outside the kit. The
scatter's trees and background buildings (`lib/world/scatterShapes.ts`) are
built the kit's way instead: smooth or chamfered normals, the kit material,
and the same pushed line, `OUTLINE.part` wide at a landmark's scale.

Use `outline={false}` for tiny details (glazing bars, door panels, hangers)
and faces set flush into another part: a line on everything turns into noise.

## What every landmark gets for free

- **A contact shadow.** `Places` draws a soft round shadow under every
  landmark (`ContactShadow`): the ground multiplied toward the look's shade
  colour, so blue-violet by day and nearly gone at night, solid in the middle
  and fading to nothing at the edge, bent to follow the planet's curve. It is
  0.8 of the place's allowed radius by default (`shadowRadius`). A model sets
  its own radius, or none, with a property on its component:
  `MyLandmark.shadow = 0.8`, or `MyLandmark.shadow = false` for something that
  floats. It is never larger than the allowed radius, so neighbours' shadows
  never overlap.
- **Painted surfaces.** Every kit surface is a little deeper and cooler at
  the ground and a little lighter toward the top (about 7 percent from the
  ground to 2.2 units up, `PAINT`), measured in the landmark's own space, so
  it reads as painted rather than as flat CG. It never brightens anything past
  white, so it never feeds the bloom.
- **Glass and lamps that follow the day.** See `Glass` and `Lit` above.
  Window glass reads as glass by day and as lit windows at night, and lamps
  are off by day and on at night, without the landmark doing anything.

## Motion

Wrap moving parts in `Bob` (float up and down), `Spin` (turn about the
upright), `Sway` (rock about an axis: tails, hanging signs; a flag is a
`Flag`) or `Drop({ fall })` (fall `fall` straight down on a slow loop,
appearing at the top and vanishing at the foot so the loop never pops: a
coin into a slot; `DROP`, `dropPose`). Each turns its
children about its own origin, placed by its `at`, so build the children
around the pivot. They run from the shared clock, `lookUniforms.time`, which
stands still under reduced motion, so they stop with it. For any other
motion, read `lookUniforms.time.value` in `useFrame`; never a clock of your
own.

## Batching and the budget

`Places` wraps every landmark in `Batched`: its still kit parts are merged
into one mesh per material, so a landmark of a hundred parts and their
outlines draws in about twenty calls. Anything inside `Bob`, `Spin`, `Sway` or `Drop`
stays live, and so do a `Flag`, a `Picture`, a `Beam`, a `Signal`, a `Flame` and `Water`, which
have shaders or attributes of their own. Each kit part tells the batch when it
mounts, changes (its geometry, colour, place or outline) or unmounts, and so
do the composites (`Roof`, `Window`, `Door`) and the motion helpers when
their own `at` or `rot` changes; the batch is merged again after that commit
(`Batcher`), so editing a landmark under Fast Refresh shows the edit. One
thing it does not watch: a plain `<group>` of yours moving its children. Keep
groups still, or put moving ones in a motion helper.

Never write to a kit mesh's `userData`. react-three-fiber hands every kit
mesh the same marker object, so a write to one is a write to all; the markers
are frozen so that a write throws.

The world is drawn on phones and in software in the tests, and nineteen
landmarks add up. The budget is **380,000 triangles for all nineteen
landmarks together** (`PLANET_TRIANGLE_BUDGET`, counted over what each
place shows; every place has its own landmark now) and **at most 24,000 for any one**,
outlines included (`TRIANGLE_BUDGET`); both are tested over the registry.
Draw calls have a budget too (R40): **at most `DRAW_BUDGET` for one landmark
once batched, `PLANET_DRAW_BUDGET` for all nineteen**, tested beside the
triangles. A batched landmark makes one call per material its still parts use
(each colour, and each line width) plus one per live part (a moving part, a
picture, water, rain), so share colours: one dark for every near-black detail,
not five. The contact shadow `Places` draws under each landmark (one call
each) is not counted in either. The
generic building (the fallback no place uses) is 9,000 to 16,000 (one to
four storeys). In development
every landmark logs its count once in the console, and warns if it is over
the cap. Spend triangles where the camera looks (the front, the
silhouette), use `outline={false}` on tiny details, and prefer one
well-proportioned part over three small ones. The part table above says
roughly what each costs.

## Materials and disposal

The kit's materials are shared (`celMaterial`, `outlineShellMaterial`,
`outlineMaterial`, `glowMaterial`, `glassMaterial`, `contactShadowMaterial` in
`../materials.ts`). Every mesh that uses
one passes `dispose={null}`, or react-three-fiber frees the shared material
under every other landmark when one unmounts. The kit's parts already do.
Geometry and materials a landmark makes itself, outside the kit, it disposes
of on unmount (`useEffect` cleanup).

## Reviewing a close-up

Build the site (`npm run build && npm run start`), fix the clock and open the
place: `/places/<id>` once the canvas wrapper reports
`data-rig-mode="focus"`. Look at it at 14:00, 18:00 and 23:00, at full
quality (do not set `window.__worldRenderScale`). Then check:

- [ ] It says what it is without its label.
- [ ] The masses are in proportion; nothing looks like a stack of toy blocks.
- [ ] Every edge a real object has is bevelled and catches a soft highlight.
- [ ] Lines are thin and even, indigo, not black; no line is doubled or broken.
- [ ] Shadows are coloured (blue by day, violet at sunset, indigo at night).
- [ ] All three levels of detail are there, and the props reward a close look.
- [ ] Colours are from the palette and the district colour; nothing reads as a
      flat primary.
- [ ] By day the windows read as glass with the sky in it, not as coloured
      rectangles; at dusk they start to warm; at night they glow and catch the
      bloom, and nothing else does.
- [ ] It sits in its contact shadow: grounded by day, not floating.
- [ ] It reads from the sides and back as the camera circles.
- [ ] It stays in its footprint and under the triangle budget (no console
      warning in development), stands on the ground, and nothing floats or
      pokes through.
- [ ] By day no lamp is lit; at night they are.
- [ ] It holds still under reduced motion.
